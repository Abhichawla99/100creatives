What makes golf and club apparel imagery different

A golf polo is sold three ways, and each one wants a different picture. The direct-to-consumer shopper wants to see it on a swing and at the turn with a drink. The green-grass pro-shop buyer wants every colorway, flat and color-true, so they can order the right mix of colors and sizes. And if you make logo merchandise, the club's merchandise committee wants to see its crest sitting straight on your chest panel.

On top of that, three things come from outside the brand. Clubs and famous courses own their names, crests and some landmarks, and a campaign shot or generated at a recognizable place can borrow that without asking. Performance claims such as UPF ratings sit on a test standard, so the number in the ad is a claim you have to be able to back. And the year has a fixed peak: buyers meet brands at the PGA Show in Orlando in late January, so the imagery for spring wholesale has to exist before the direct-to-consumer season starts.

The sections below take those in order, then the frames that need a real shoot, then the plan.

Club crests and famous courses: whose name is in the frame?

Golf has a court case on this exact problem. Tour 18 built a course in Texas that copied famous holes, including the 18th at Harbour Town with its lighthouse, and advertised them using the original courses' names. In 1998 the Fifth Circuit held that Tour 18's prominent use of those names in advertising, on menus and on tee signs suggested "affiliation, sponsorship, or approval", found the Harbour Town lighthouse protectable as a mark, and required prominent, bold disclaimers on the front of Tour 18's ads. It also held that the layout of the Pebble Beach 14th and Pinehurst 3rd holes was not protectable on its own (Pebble Beach Co. v. Tour 18 I, Ltd., 5th Cir. 1998, checked October 2026).

Our reading for imagery, which is not legal advice: a fairway is free, but a landmark, a course name or a club crest that tells the viewer where the photo was taken is not. Three practical rules follow.

Shooting at a real club. Get a location agreement that says the images may be used in paid ads, for how long, and whether the club's name can appear. A club can allow a shoot and still say no to its name in an ad.

Logo merchandise. If you embroider club crests for pro shops, a photo of a member's crest on your polo is the club's mark in your ad. Get each club's written approval for the specific uses (product page, ads, linesheet) or show your own logo and a blank chest.

Generated courses. Image models will happily produce a lighthouse behind a green or a cliff-top par 3 that looks like one famous hole. Brief generic settings instead (links, parkland, desert, a clubhouse terrace with no signage) and reject any render that reads as a specific place.

UPF and performance claims: the number in the ad has to match the label

Golf and tennis brands sell on sun protection, stretch and cooling, and those words end up in headlines and on badges in the corner of an ad. UPF has a standard behind it. AATCC Test Method 183 is the method used to measure UV transmission through fabric, and ASTM D6603 is the companion specification for labeling UV-protective textiles (Textile World, August 2025, checked October 2026). Under the ASTM labeling approach, fabric is laundered and exposed to UV before its rating is assigned, ratings step up in fives from 15, and "50+" marks anything above 50; in the US it is the FTC's job to monitor such claims (University of Arizona, UV-protective clothing labeling, checked October 2026).

For the creative, that means three checks. The UPF number in the ad is the number on the tested label for that exact fabric, not the best number in the line. Every size of the ad carries the same wording, so a story crop doesn't drop "UPF 50+" to "sun protection" on one colorway and keep it on another. And no image tool or designer adds a badge, a seal or a "cooling" icon the product team hasn't signed off. Claims about moisture-wicking or temperature control are claims too; if your product team can't show a test result, describe how the fabric feels instead.

The fabric also has to be shown honestly. A performance polo sells on how it moves through a swing and how the collar holds after nine holes. AI image models render "athletic fabric" in general and struggle with a specific knit's sheen, weight and stretch recovery. Shoot the movement frames on the real garment, and use them as the reference every later image is checked against.

Keeping the collar and the logo the same in every frame

The details club apparel shoppers notice are small: how the collar rolls, where the placket stops, whether the chest logo sits the same distance below the shoulder seam in every image. Generated variants drift on exactly those details, and a polo whose logo moves an inch between the ad and the product page looks like a different product.

A plan that works back from the PGA Show

The 2027 PGA Show runs January 26 to 29 at the Orange County Convention Center in Orlando, with Demo Day on January 26 and the show floor open January 27 to 29 (PGA Show, Overview & Hours, checked October 2026). The order below is our plan, not an industry standard, and the dates depend on when your samples land and how long your team takes to review.

Before any image. Confirm the tested UPF and any other claim on each label, the club logos you have permission to show, and whether any shoot location will let its name appear. Complete the brand intake: visual language, model preferences, colorways and export specs.

The shoot, as soon as samples arrive. Swing and walking frames, collar and logo close-ups, and color-true flats of every colorway. The flats feed the linesheet buyers see at the show; the movement frames feed everything after it.

The first ad set. A small set of concepts that differ by reason to buy: the fabric on a hot day, the look from course to clubhouse, the new colorways, the gift. The review settles direction before volume. Our guide on how to brief an agency for AI creative lists what to send.

Spring launch. Finished ads in every placement size, with the UPF wording identical in each one. Our guide to static ads for Meta covers sizes and safe zones for each placement.

The season. Member-guest, Father's Day and holiday versions, made ahead from approved images and switched on as dates pass. For how many ads a season like this needs, see how many ad creatives you need.

Frequently asked questions

Can I show a golf club's logo or crest in my ads?
Only with the club's written permission. A club crest is a trademark, and putting it on a model in your ad suggests the club endorses your brand. If you sell logo polos to clubs, get each club's approval for the specific use: product pages, ads, wholesale linesheets, and how long. For general ads, show your own logo or a blank chest. This is our reading, not legal advice.
Can AI generate a famous golf course behind my apparel?
Avoid recognizable courses. In Pebble Beach Co. v. Tour 18 (1998), the Fifth Circuit held that a replica course's prominent use of famous course names in advertising suggested affiliation, and it protected the Harbour Town lighthouse as a mark. A generated scene that clearly reads as one famous hole carries the same risk. Brief generic links, parkland or desert courses instead.
Can I put UPF 50+ on a golf polo ad?
Only if the fabric was tested and the garment is labeled that way. In the US, UPF is measured with AATCC Test Method 183 and labeled under ASTM D6603, and the FTC is the agency that polices the claim. Copy the exact rating from the label into every ad size, and never let a designer or an image tool add a UPF badge the label doesn't carry.
Which golf apparel images need a real photo shoot?
The swing and walking frames that show stretch and drape on a real body, close-ups of the collar, placket and embroidered logo, the color-true flats your pro-shop buyers order from, and any frame shot at a real club with its permission. Generated imagery fits the work around them: more course and clubhouse settings, every ad placement size, and seasonal versions of approved looks.
When should a golf apparel brand have new imagery ready for the PGA Show?
Before the show. The 2027 PGA Show runs January 26 to 29 in Orlando, with Demo Day on January 26, and buyers judge the line from your linesheet and booth images. Shoot linesheet flats and fit frames as soon as samples arrive, then build the spring direct-to-consumer ads from those approved photos so they can launch soon after the show.
