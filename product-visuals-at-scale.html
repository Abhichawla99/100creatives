The numbers

What "at scale" means in images

Count per product, then multiply. A store page wants several images of each product. In the Baymard Institute's product page study (2017), 56% of test users started by exploring the images, and 42% tried to judge a product's size and scale from them.

Each ad placement wants its own crop too. Meta calls for 1440 x 1800 at 4:5 in feeds and 1440 x 2560 at 9:16 in Stories and Reels (Meta Ads Guide), Shopify recommends 2048 x 2048 for square product images (Shopify Help Center), and TikTok suggests 3 to 5 different creatives per ad group (TikTok Ads Help Center). All were checked in September 2026.

A worked example, not a benchmark: 50 products with 6 finished images each is 300 images. At our average of 8 renders per keeper, that is about 2,400 renders. On Nano Banana 2 at $0.067 a render (Gemini API pricing), the model fees come to about $161. Someone also has to look at all 2,400.

The system

Five things that make scale possible

1. A reference library, one set per product

Photograph every side that will show, the label flat and straight, and close-ups of texture or hardware. A phone works if the photos are sharp, evenly lit and true to color. Every later image of that product reuses the set.

2. Brand rules, written once

Write the look down in terms an image model can follow: hex codes for the palette and background, the direction and hardness of the light, the lens and the grain. Our kept prompts on Nano Banana models described light as something falling on a surface and gave grain its own sentence. Rules written once apply to every product after.

3. A cast, approved once and reused

For anything worn or held, approve one character sheet and reuse it. On our surf job the sheet took 20 attempts, a cost worth paying once per model instead of once per product. In Overs, earlier character sheets become a talent library, and packaged products can use a cast too.

4. Shot templates with fixed sentences

On our trousers job, the backdrop and lighting sentences were reused word for word across the store photos. Only the framing and pose changed from photo to photo, and the camera sentence changed once, for the fabric close-up. Fixed sentences hold the backdrop and the light steady across a set.

5. Review that feeds back

Every approve, change or reject needs a reason, and the reasons need to reach the next prompt. In Overs they become brand memory, and a note that fixed an approved photo is remembered for later photos.

How do brands make product photos at scale?

By reusing four things across every product: one set of reference photos per product, brand rules written once as hex codes and light and lens settings, an approved character sheet for anything worn or held, and shot templates whose backdrop and light sentences stay fixed. After that, review time sets the pace. In our hand-built campaigns it took about 8 renders per keeper.

How many images does a product need?

It depends on where it sells. A store page wants several angles and at least one image that shows scale, since Baymard's 2017 study found 42% of test users tried to judge size from product images. Each ad placement also wants its own crop, such as 4:5 for Meta feeds and 9:16 for Stories and Reels.

What does it cost to make 300 product images with AI?

As a worked example, 300 finished images at our average of 8 renders per keeper is about 2,400 renders. On Nano Banana 2 at $0.067 a render, that is about $161 in model fees, plus a tool plan such as Overs Pro at $29 a month. The larger cost is the time it takes to review 2,400 renders.

What slows AI product photography down at scale?

Review. Model fees stay small, but every render needs someone to compare it with the product and the brand. In our four hand-built campaigns, the studio jobs took 3 to 4 renders per keeper, while one model carried across three scenes took 13.6. Plan catalog work around one backdrop, one light and fixed prompt sentences, like the studio jobs.

Can one AI model appear across a whole catalog?

Yes, if you lock the person first. Make a character sheet, one model shown from several angles in the exact product, approve it, and send it as the first reference in every photo with a person. On one of our surf jobs the sheet took 20 attempts, so it pays to reuse it. Overs keeps earlier sheets as a talent library.
