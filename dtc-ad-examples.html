DTC Static Ad Examples,
and Why They Work

By Abhi Chawla, founder · Last updated: 2026-09-14

Most of our client work is under NDA, so every example on this page is a spec concept: work made for a brand that did not commission it. There are 25 static ads for three consumer brands, each set with notes on why the layout works. New to the format? Start with what a static ad is.

How to use this swipe file

A static ad is one still image, usually the product, a headline and a call to action. A new angle only needs a new layout, which makes statics a practical way to test many angles on Meta, and the layout decisions below carry over to almost any product.

The 25 concepts cover three categories: 10 for a Greek yogurt, 9 for a colostrum supplement and 6 for a wine brand. Each set comes with notes on what the layout does, from type hierarchy and product placement to color, proof and the CTA. The principles behind the notes are in what makes a good static ad.

100 Creatives made these concepts. None of the three brands commissioned them, and none of the concepts ran as paid ads, so they show layout and copy structure and say nothing about results. Some include proof lines, such as customer counts or superiority claims, that a real brand would need to substantiate before running them.

Food & Beverage

Chobani

Spec work, not commissioned by Chobani

Ten spec concepts for Chobani Strawberry Greek Yogurt, built around one product: the cup, fresh strawberries and a swirl of yogurt. Most concepts pair a short serif headline in dark green with a pink or cream background and a pill-shaped CTA. The hook changes from ad to ad while the product stays constant.

Why These Work

  • The product sits at the center of every frame. All ten concepts put the cup in the middle, with strawberries and yogurt moving around it. Nothing covers the pack, so a viewer knows what is being sold before reading a word. For food, the motion of the yogurt and fruit does the appetite work that a flat pack shot can't.
  • One headline, one subline, one CTA. Nearly every concept runs a short headline ("Power Meets Pleasure.", "Start Bright. Stay Full.", "Catch the Flavor."), a single supporting line and a button such as "Try it today." The eye moves from headline to product to button, and there is nothing else to read.
  • A tight palette that repeats. Strawberry pink, cream and the dark green of the wordmark on the cup appear in every frame, and four concepts set an oversized word (PROTEIN, STRAWBERRY, PAUSE, REAL FRUIT) behind the product as texture. The repetition makes the ten ads read as one campaign while each hook stays different.
  • Proof gets its own shapes. One concept turns review stars and a quote into speech bubbles made of yogurt; another frames the cup with a laurel wreath and the line "Trusted by millions." In a live campaign, a line like that needs documentation behind it before it runs.

Supplements & Wellness

ARMRA

Spec work, not commissioned by ARMRA

Nine spec concepts for ARMRA Colostrum, a powdered supplement sold in a plain white tub. The set tests how far a white package can carry an ad when the background color, a round badge and a bold headline do the rest.

Why These Work

  • A white tub on saturated color. Eight of the nine concepts put the tub on flat periwinkle, chartreuse, sky blue, peach, black or white backgrounds, which makes the package the brightest object in the frame. A round badge or color dot from the same palette ties the nine ads together.
  • Credibility cues as badges. Round stickers ("Doctor Formulated", "Pure & Potent", "Maximum Potency") and sublines ("Physician-Developed Colostrum", "Immune Support Backed by Science") give a reason to trust the product in a few words. In a real campaign, each of these claims needs substantiation, and a comparison line such as "30% More Bioactives Than Any Other Brand" needs proof the brand can show. The FTC's advertising guide for small businesses says advertisers must have evidence to back up their claims.
  • Movement inside a still frame. Powder pours from a scoop, bursts across the surface or dissolves in a glass of water, and citrus or peach halves sit beside the tub. The motion cues show the product in use and hint at freshness without a video.

Beverage & Lifestyle

Barefoot

Spec work, not commissioned by Barefoot

Six spec concepts for Barefoot, a wine brand with a casual, playful voice. Each one puts a bottle of White Zinfandel or rosé at the center of a crafted scene and sells an occasion, from a party to a hot afternoon.

Why These Work

  • Crafted, tactile backgrounds. Cut-paper hearts, a torn-paper sunburst, gold foil, a pop-art halftone burst, fresh strawberries and leaves, and rippling water. Each background has a texture you can almost feel, which keeps a flat digital ad from looking like a stock template.
  • Headlines that sell the occasion. "Pulse of the Party", "Pour Out the Sunshine" and "Joy Starts Here" describe a mood instead of the wine's taste. For a casual bottle, the occasion gives people a reason to pick it up, and the short, relaxed CTAs ("Grab a bottle", "Find yours today") match that voice.
  • The bottle stays the hero. Every concept centers the bottle, often lit from within or filled with the scene itself, such as ice and strawberries or swirling color. The shape stays recognizable even when the label is partly hidden. One concept carries the line "America's most awarded wine", a claim a live ad would need to source.

Patterns across the spec concepts

5 Patterns Across
All 25 Concepts

01

One Message Per Ad

None of the 25 concepts tries to carry more than one idea. The Chobani spec concepts sell taste and protein, the ARMRA spec concepts sell trust and a daily routine, and the Barefoot spec concepts sell a good time, one idea per ad. Meta's research found people spend 1.7 seconds on average with a piece of mobile feed content (Facebook IQ, 2016), and one idea is about as much as that time allows.

02

Visual Hierarchy You Can Read in Two Seconds

Every concept has a clear reading order: headline, product, CTA. The headline is the largest text in almost every concept, the product carries the most visual weight, and the CTA sits where the reading order ends. Treat a static ad like a billboard: a few words, one image, one instruction.

03

Color Sets the Mood Before the Copy Does

Before anyone reads a word, color sets the tone. The Chobani spec concepts use pink and cream with dark green type and read as fresh and sweet. The ARMRA spec concepts put a white tub on saturated flat color and read as clean and clinical. The Barefoot spec concepts use bright, crafted scenes and read as festive. Choose the palette as early as the headline, because it decides whether the ad feels like the brand.

04

The Product Is Never an Afterthought

In all 25 concepts the product is visible and large, and in most of them the label is readable. Lifestyle imagery, dense copy or an overly clever idea can bury a product, and if a viewer can't picture what they would be buying, the headline and the offer have nothing to attach to.

05

Proof Gets Its Own Space in the Design

Ratings, badges, customer counts and quote bubbles get their own shapes and space instead of sitting in small print. The ARMRA spec concepts use round badges, and the Chobani spec concepts use a laurel wreath and speech bubbles. Every proof element in a live ad has to be true and documented before it runs.

Frequently Asked Questions

Everything You Need
to Know About DTC Ads

What makes a good DTC ad?

A good DTC static ad makes one point at a glance. It leads with a benefit headline, shows the product large enough to recognize, adds one piece of proof the brand can document, and ends with a single clear call to action. Meta's research found people spend 1.7 seconds on average with a piece of mobile feed content, so the whole ad has to read in about that time.

How many ad creatives should I test?

There is no universal number. How many new creatives you need depends on your spend, the number of audiences you run and how quickly ads fatigue in your account. Test different angles, such as a benefit, a review or a comparison, before testing colors and fonts, and treat testing as a weekly routine. Our ad creative testing framework walks through the process.

What size should DTC ads be?

On Meta, Facebook and Instagram feeds take a 4:5 image, and Meta recommends 1440 x 1800 pixels. Stories and Reels take 9:16 at 1440 x 2560 pixels, with roughly the top 14%, the bottom 35% and 6% of each side kept free of text and logos. Carousel cards on Facebook Feed are 1:1 and at least 1080 x 1080 pixels. Specs checked September 2026 in Meta's Ads Guide.

Do static ads outperform video for DTC?

Neither format wins by default. Static ads are faster and cheaper to produce and easier to test in volume, which suits offers, launches, reviews and comparisons. Video suits demonstrations, texture and people talking to camera. Which format performs better depends on the product and the angle, so test the same idea in both and compare cost per purchase in your own account.
