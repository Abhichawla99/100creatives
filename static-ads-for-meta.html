Why brands still run
static ads on Meta

A static ad delivers its whole message in one glance, and a new angle only needs a new layout. That makes statics a cheap way to test ideas in Facebook and Instagram feeds.

The case for static

Video gets a lot of attention on Meta, and static images still do jobs that video can't do as cheaply. A static loads as a single image, needs no sound, and shows the product, the headline and the offer at the same time.

That matters because feed attention is short. Meta's own research found people spend 1.7 seconds on average with a piece of mobile feed content (Facebook IQ, 2016). A well-built static communicates inside that window, before anyone decides whether to watch or read.

The economics favor volume. A video concept needs footage and an edit, while a static concept needs a layout, so testing several angles on the same product costs less. Meta's Advantage+ creative can also generate and enhance variations of single image ads once they are uploaded.

Static and video do different jobs. The side-by-side, including what AI video can do for product ads, is in static vs video ads.

Anatomy of a Meta static

What makes a strong
Meta static ad

Composition and Hierarchy

An effective static moves the eye from the hook to the product to the offer in a fixed order. The product is the hero, and the background exists to create contrast around it.

Put the headline where the eye lands first, usually near the top, and make sure the headline and the product image make the same point. In Stories and Reels, keep text and logos out of roughly the top 14%, the bottom 35% and 6% of each side, the areas Meta suggests leaving clear.

CTA, Color, and Contrast

Meta shows the call-to-action button you choose with the ad, and the image itself still needs a cue toward the next step: a CTA shape, an arrow, a gaze or a contrasting color block. Use the same words in both places so the ad asks for one thing.

Color in the Meta feed works by context. Your ad sits between personal photos, memes and news, and the ads that get noticed create enough visual tension to interrupt the scroll while staying recognizably on-brand. High saturation suits some categories and muted palettes suit others; in every category, contrast with the posts around the ad is what gets it seen.
