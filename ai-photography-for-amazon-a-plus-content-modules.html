AI photography for Amazon A+ Content modules

AI-made images work in Amazon A+ Content for lifestyle scenes, Brand Story backgrounds and mood frames, as long as the product in them matches what the customer receives. For comparison charts, hotspots and anything that demonstrates how the product works, use real photos.

By Abhi Chawla, founder · Last updated: 2026-11-13

100 Creatives makes product imagery, including the images that go into A+ modules. We don't build or upload A+ Content. This page says where we fit and where someone else is the better hire.

The basics

What is Amazon A+ Content, and who can use it?

A+ Content is the part of an Amazon product detail page where a brand adds its own images, text placements and comparison charts. Amazon describes it as a way to share your brand's story on product detail pages "by adding videos, enhanced images, customized text placements, shoppable comparison charts, and more" (Sell on Amazon, A+ Content, checked November 2026).

The same page sets out who can use it:

  • A Professional selling account. Amazon lists it at $39.99 a month plus selling fees.
  • A brand in Amazon Brand Registry. You need a Brand Representative or Reseller role on a brand enrolled in Brand Registry, or generic products in your catalog. Brand Registry itself is free, and enrolling needs a pending or registered trademark for your brand name or logo from a country with an Amazon store (Sell on Amazon, Brand Registry, checked November 2026).
  • Brand-owned ASINs only. A+ Content applies to your own brand's listings.

A+ Content is free for sellers who meet those rules. Premium A+ "is available to registered brands that meet certain criteria", which Amazon's A+ Content design guide (checked November 2026) doesn't spell out. Check your account in Seller Central before you plan Premium modules.

On sales, Amazon's A+ page says "Basic A+ Content can increase sales by up to 8%" and "well-implemented Premium A+ Content can increase sales by up to 20%", with a footnote citing Amazon internal data. Amazon doesn't publish how it measured them, so treat them as ceilings rather than a forecast for your listing.

The modules

What images do A+ Content modules need?

Basic A+ allows up to five modules per ASIN, and Premium allows up to seven (A+ Content design guide, checked November 2026). Each module is a template, and the template decides what image it takes.

The rules

Amazon's rules for A+ Content images

Amazon's A+ Content design guide (checked November 2026) sets these rules for what goes in your images:

  • Files no larger than 2MB, at the dimensions the module asks for. Avoid low-resolution or animated images.
  • Keep text out of images. Amazon says to avoid embedding text in images so mobile shoppers can read the details on small screens. Put the words in the module's text fields.
  • No "best-selling" or "top-rated" claims in text or images.
  • No pricing, promotions, shipping details, QR codes or hyperlinks. A "Limited" badge or a "Shop now" button from an ad frame has to come off.
  • No warranty or guarantee information in text placements.
  • Never reference or compare against competitors. Your comparison chart compares your own products.
  • Back up awards and endorsements with the certifying body and year.
  • Own the rights to every image, video and line of text you use.

The same guide recommends "professional product photos along with lifestyle imagery showing items in action", and the A+ Content Manager lets you preview desktop and mobile versions before you submit. Amazon's product photo guide (checked November 2026) adds a reason to keep everything accurate: "Appealing yet accurate product photography also helps keep return rates in check."

AI imagery

Can you use AI images in A+ Content?

Amazon's public A+ pages don't ban AI-made images. Amazon's own tools generate content: the design guide says to "explore modules with the 'AI Ready' badge" in the A+ Content Manager, where selected text and image fields can be filled with automatically generated content. The rules that limit AI imagery are the ones above. The product has to be accurate, you need the rights to the image, and claims can't sneak in through a picture.

Where AI-made imagery fits. Lifestyle scenes, settings and backgrounds: the tub on a kitchen counter, the jacket on a trail, a wide Brand Story banner. The product in them only has to match the real pack, and AI makes it cheap to try several settings before you pick one.

Where it doesn't. Anything a shopper uses to check the product:

  • Comparison chart thumbnails, where every SKU has to be the right color, size and pack.
  • Hotspot images, where each tagged feature has to exist on the real thing.
  • Close-ups of labels, ingredients panels or fine print. AI image models redraw small text and can change words or numbers.
  • Scale, fit and "what's in the box" frames.
  • Demonstrations. If the module says the bottle is leakproof, a generated image of a dry bag proves nothing.

Our guide to when you still need a real photo shoot goes through these cases in more detail.

Budget time for rejects. When we built four campaigns by hand earlier in 2026 with AI image models, 361 renders produced 45 keepers, about 8 renders per usable image. Someone has to look at every one of them against the real product.

Planning

How to plan an A+ image set

  1. Pick the modules first. Lay out the five (or seven) modules for your hero ASIN in the A+ Content Manager and note the image size each one shows. That list is your shot list.
  2. Get one real reference of the product. A clean, straight-on photo of the final pack, or the print file, is what every AI frame gets checked against.
  3. Shoot what has to be real. Comparison packshots, hotspot images, labels and demonstrations.
  4. Make the scenes. Lifestyle frames, larger Premium images and Brand Story backgrounds, all with text left off.
  5. Check every keeper at full size against the real product: color, shape, label, closures and any printed number.
  6. Export under 2MB at each module's dimensions and preview on desktop and mobile before you submit.

The image gallery above A+ has its own rules. Amazon requires images between 500 and 10,000 pixels on the longest side, prefers more than 1,000 pixels on each side for zoom, and recommends at least six images (Sell on Amazon, product photo guide, checked November 2026).
