When does a brand still need a real photo shoot?

Book a real shoot when the ad has to prove something (a demonstration, a result, a real customer's words), when a specific real person has to appear, or when an image model still gets your product wrong after better references and a stronger model. Packshots, lifestyle scenes and on-model sets with a consistent AI model can be made without a camera. Anything the ad offers as proof needs one.

By Abhi Chawla, founder · Last updated: 2026-09-24

100 Creatives makes its images in Overs, an AI photo tool built by our founder, Abhi Chawla. Both are brands of 2587689 Alberta Ltd. This page is about where that tool, and any AI tool, should stop.

The short version

Shoot it when the image is evidence: a demonstration of what the product does, a before-and-after or other result, a real customer's testimonial, or a specific real person. Make it with AI when the image is a scene or a setting and the product in it matches the real one. When in doubt, ask what a viewer would believe the photo proves.

Demonstrations

If the ad demonstrates, shoot the real thing

An ad that shows what a product does is making a claim with a picture. In FTC v. Colgate-Palmolive, decided April 5, 1965, the US Supreme Court agreed with the FTC that a shaving-cream commercial was deceptive when it passed off a plexiglass mock-up covered in sand as sandpaper being shaved. The Court drew the line at proof. A mock-up offered as visual proof of a product claim was deceptive, while a prop that isn't offered as proof, such as mashed potatoes standing in for ice cream, was not covered by the order.

We read AI images the same way. A lifestyle scene around a product that matches the real one is a setting. An image that shows the product doing something, such as a stain lifting, a surface repelling water or a drink staying cold, is offered as proof, so shoot the real product doing the real thing.

Real customers

Testimonials need real people

The FTC's Endorsement Guides say an endorsement must reflect the honest opinions or experience of the endorser, and an endorser presented as a user must have used the product. Its rule on fake reviews and testimonials, in force since October 21, 2024, bans fake reviews and testimonials, including ones attributed to people who don't exist, and the FTC's announcement names AI-generated fake reviews as an example.

An AI face beside a quote presents a person as a customer. Use a real customer, with permission, and a real photo.

Results

Results need the real result

A before-and-after, a skin result or a fit claim tells the viewer what will happen to them. In the UK, the ASA ruled in 2021 that two tanning ads using beauty filters were misleading, and CAP advises against filters that relate directly to the product and are likely to exaggerate its effect (ASA, February 2021). CAP's June 2026 guidance says the advertising code applies to AI-made ads too (CAP, June 2026).

Health claims carry their own platform rules. Our page on health and wellness ad creative covers Meta's.

Real people

A specific person has to be there

A founder, an athlete or a creator the audience knows has to be photographed, or has to agree in writing to any digital likeness of them. A fully synthetic AI model is nobody in particular, and the rules for it are about disclosure.

New York has required anyone producing a commercial ad to conspicuously disclose a synthetic performer they know is in it since June 9, 2026, with fines of $1,000 for a first violation and $5,000 after that (New York Governor's office). Amazon requires the metadata keyword "contains-synthetic-performer" on main images that show photorealistic AI-generated people (Amazon product image guide).

Hard products

When the model keeps getting the product wrong

Some jobs take far more tries than others. In our four hand-built campaigns, packaged gummies took 3.2 renders per keeper and trousers in a studio 3.6, while one surf model carried across studio, cliff and ocean scenes took 13.6. If a product still comes back wrong after better references (every side, the label flat and straight) and a stronger model such as Nano Banana Pro, which Overs lists as its best model for fine detail and matching references, photograph it.

The model fees won't be what pushes you to a shoot. At $0.211 a render on GPT Image 2 at high quality (OpenAI's image generation guide), 20 tries cost about $4.22. What pushes you to a shoot is accuracy, because a photo a shopper compares with the box in their hands has to match the box.

The hybrid

Shoot once, extend with AI

A shoot and AI work well together when the shoot captures what the AI will need later. On shoot day, photograph:

  • every side of the product, with the label flat and straight;
  • close-ups of texture, stitching and hardware;
  • the backs of garments, because a model can only redraw what it has seen;
  • the real people, with a release that covers any digital likeness you plan to make.

Then the shoot covers the proof, and AI covers new scenes, seasons and placements from the same references.

Frequently asked
questions

When do you need a real photo shoot instead of AI?

When the image is evidence: a demonstration of what the product does, a before-and-after or other result, a real customer's testimonial, or a specific real person such as a founder or athlete. Book one too when a model keeps getting the product wrong after better references and a stronger model. Settings, packshots and scenes can be made with AI.

Can I use AI images in a product demonstration ad?

Not as the demonstration itself. In FTC v. Colgate-Palmolive (1965), the US Supreme Court agreed that passing off a mock-up as the real thing in a demonstration was deceptive, while a prop not offered as proof was treated differently. If the ad shows what the product does, show the real product doing it.

Can AI-generated people appear in testimonial ads?

Not as customers. The FTC's Endorsement Guides say an endorser presented as a user must have used the product, and its 2024 rule bans fake reviews and testimonials, naming AI-generated ones as an example. Use a real customer, with permission, and a real photo.

Do ads with AI models need a disclosure?

In some places. New York has required a conspicuous disclosure when a commercial ad includes a synthetic performer since June 9, 2026. Amazon requires a metadata keyword on main images with photorealistic AI people, and the EU AI Act's transparency rules apply from August 2, 2026. Check each market you run in.

Is a real photo shoot more expensive than AI?

For most images, yes. Model fees ran about $0.27 to $1.69 per usable photo at our average of 8 renders per keeper, while soona publishes ~$39 a photo plus a ~$149 studio fee per booking. The reasons to book a shoot are proof, real people and accuracy.

Can you combine a photo shoot with AI images?

Yes. Shoot what has to be real, and capture what AI will need later: every side of the product, the label flat and straight, close-ups of texture, the backs of garments, and real people with a release that covers any digital likeness. AI can then extend the shoot into new scenes, seasons and placements.
