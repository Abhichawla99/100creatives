Static ads, defined

What is a
static ad?

By Abhi Chawla, founder · Last updated: 2026-09-14

A static ad is a paid ad made of one still image, with no motion or sound. It usually shows the product, a short headline and a call to action, and it runs in placements such as the Facebook and Instagram feeds, Stories, and the Google Display Network. Every spec on this page links to the platform page we checked in September 2026.

Definitions

What is static advertising?

Static advertising is advertising built on fixed images. Online, it means image ads in social feeds, Stories and display networks. Offline, the same term covers print ads, posters and static billboards, the ones that stay put instead of rotating through spots like a digital screen. A static advertisement and a static ad are the same thing.

The ad platforms each use their own name for the format. Meta's Ads Guide calls it an image ad. Google Ads asks for image assets in Performance Max and responsive display campaigns. TikTok runs images in its feed through Carousel Ads.

The image file itself is the static creative. One concept usually ships as a set of static creatives, one per shape: a 4:5 portrait for feeds, a 9:16 vertical for Stories and Reels, and 1.91:1 landscape and 1:1 square versions for Google.

TikTok: images run as Carousel Ads

TikTok's in-feed ad specifications cover video only. For images, TikTok Ads Manager offers Carousel Ads, which TikTok's help center describes as a format that supports images for in-feed ads. The About Carousel Ads article lists Traffic, Web Conversions, App Promotion, Lead Generation, Product Sales (Catalog) and Brand Auction Reach as supported ad types.

Neither article lists dimensions or file limits for carousel images, so we have left them out. Confirm them in TikTok Ads Manager before you export.

Google: Performance Max and display image assets

Google's overview of ad specs names 1200 x 628 (1.91:1) and 1200 x 1200 (1:1) as the most common Google Ads image sizes. Performance Max asks for both, plus an optional 4:5 vertical, and Google describes Performance Max assets as serving across YouTube, Display, Search, Discover, Gmail and Maps.

Anatomy

Anatomy of a high-converting
static ad

People glance at feed ads. Meta's own research found that people spend 1.7 seconds on average with a piece of mobile feed content, and that 0.25 seconds of exposure is enough for them to recall it (Facebook IQ, April 2016). The first five parts below have to work inside that glance. The sixth makes sure the placement doesn't cover them.

01

The hook

The headline carries the idea by itself. Write the benefit, the problem or the surprising fact in a few words, make it the largest type on the image, and check it on a phone held at arm's length before you approve it.

02

The product

Show exactly what the buyer gets, large enough to recognize at feed size. The pack, colorway and label should match what arrives in the box, because the product page and the delivery have to live up to the ad.

03

The offer

Give a reason to act now if one exists: a price, a bundle, a discount, free shipping. With no offer, name the next step instead. Put a deadline on the image only when the deadline is real.

04

The proof

A review quote, a star rating with its review count, an award, a press mention or a sales figure. Each one has to be true and documented. The FTC's advertising guide for small businesses says advertisers must have evidence to back up their claims.

05

The CTA

One action in plain words, set apart from the headline so it reads as the next step. Use the same words as the call-to-action button you choose in Ads Manager, so the ad asks for one thing. In Stories and Reels, keep it above the bottom safe zone.

06

The placement

Design each size on purpose instead of cropping one file. For Stories and Reels, Meta suggests keeping roughly the top 14%, the bottom 35% and 6% of each side clear of text and logos, so the interface doesn't cover them.

The design rules that sit on top of these six parts, from text hierarchy to contrast and whitespace, are in what makes a good static ad.

Static vs video

Static vs video ads: when each wins

Static ads suit messages that fit in one frame: a new product, a price or offer, a review, a comparison. Each concept is one layout per size, so testing a new angle costs one new design.

Video suits products people need to see in use: texture, fit, a routine, a before-and-after that happens over time, or a person talking to camera. It is also the default on TikTok, where the in-feed ad specifications cover video and images run as carousels.

One workable order is to test an angle as a static first, then make video for the angles that win. The longer comparison, including what AI video tools can and can't do for product ads, is in static vs video ads.

01

Lead with static

Offers, launches, reviews, comparisons and price changes. One frame, quick to swap when an offer ends.

02

Lead with video

Demos, texture, routines and people talking. Full-screen video placements such as Reels and the TikTok feed.

03

Use both

When an angle wins as a static, a video version gives the same idea more room to show the product.

Static ad
questions

What are static ads?

Static ads are paid ads made of a single still image, with no video, animation or sound. They usually combine a product image, a short headline and a call to action, and they run as image ads on Facebook, Instagram and Google, or as image carousels on TikTok. The image file behind a static ad is called the static creative, and one concept is usually exported in several sizes.

What is static advertising?

Static advertising is advertising built on fixed images instead of video or motion. Online, it covers image ads in social feeds, Stories and display networks, such as Facebook and Instagram image ads and Google Performance Max image assets. Offline, it covers print ads, posters and static billboards, the kind that stay put instead of rotating like digital screens. Brands use it because one image is quick to produce, resize and test.

Are static ads still effective on Meta?

Static ads are still fully supported on Meta. Meta's Ads Guide publishes current image specs for Facebook and Instagram feeds, Stories and Reels, and its Advantage+ creative tools generate and enhance single-image ad variations. Whether a static or a video wins in your account depends on the product, the angle and the audience, so run both formats on the same angle and budget and compare the results.

What size should a static ad be?

For Facebook and Instagram feeds, Meta recommends a 4:5 image at 1440 x 1800 pixels. For Facebook and Instagram Stories and Instagram Reels, use 9:16 at 1440 x 2560 pixels and keep roughly the top 14%, the bottom 35% and 6% of each side free of text and logos. For Google Performance Max, supply landscape 1.91:1 images at 1200 x 628 and square 1:1 images at 1200 x 1200. Specs checked September 2026.

What are the best static ad types for performance?

No static ad type wins in every account. Common types to test are a product hero with a benefit headline, a review or testimonial ad, a comparison ad, a before-and-after, a feature callout and an offer ad built around a price or discount. Choose the type that fits the angle you are testing, change one thing per test, and only use proof and claims you can document.

The ManyPixels Advanced design subscription is ~$699 a month. An in-house graphic designer earns a US median of $62,960 a year, according to the Bureau of Labor Statistics.
