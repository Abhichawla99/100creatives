Definition

What an unlimited ad creative subscription is

An unlimited ad creative subscription is a design service billed at a flat monthly rate. You can add any number of requests to the queue, and the service works on a set number of them at once. Vendors call that number active requests, slots, workstreams or daily outputs. The word unlimited describes the queue. What you receive in a month depends on how many requests are active and how long each one takes.

Most of these services sell design for every channel, with ads as one request type. Kimp's plans cover social media ads, display ads, landing pages and print. A few are built around ads: GetAds sells ad creative requests for paid acquisition teams, and Marmalaide sells a monthly count of AI-made videos and statics.

Software uses the word too. AdPyro's ~$69-a-month Forge plan includes unlimited static creatives, generated by the tool's AI, plus 40 UGC videos a month.

Mechanics

How unlimited ad creatives work in practice

01

Active requests

The number of requests in progress at once. The Creative Unlimited and Creative Kaiju's Core plan work on 1, Kimp's Graphics plan on 3 and GetAds' Scale plan on 4. Everything else waits in the queue.

02

Turnaround per request

Each service quotes a turnaround per request. Creative Kaiju quotes 48 hours. The Creative Unlimited quotes 48 hours for graphics and 2 to 4 days for video. Kimp quotes 24 hours for simple tasks and 2 to 4 days for complex ones.

03

What one request holds

Services define a request differently, so ask how many sizes, variations or copy versions fit inside one before you compare plans. ManyPixels says small tasks can share a day's output.

04

Revisions

Kimp and Creative Kaiju list unlimited revisions. ManyPixels says revisions can share a day's output with other small tasks, so check how a revision round affects your queue.

05

Hours or people instead of requests

Some services sell time. Flocksy prices by daily design hours, from 2 a day, or about 42 hours a month. ManyPixels' two top plans assign 1 or 2 part-time designers.

06

Pausing and cancelling

ManyPixels, Kimp, Penji and GetAds publish no-contract terms. ManyPixels charges $10 a month to pause and Kimp $19.95. Marmalaide asks for 30 days' notice to cancel, and Design Pickle bills quarterly or annually.

What that means for monthly volume

If a request uses its full 48-hour turnaround and the team works business days, one active request clears about 10 requests in a 21-business-day month, and 3 active requests clear about 31, before revisions. Simple requests can clear faster and complex ones slower. If your media buyer wants 30 new statics a month, count the sizes each one needs, check what a single request includes, and run that sum before you pick a plan. Neither ManyPixels' nor The Creative Unlimited's pricing page mentions a fair-use policy, so read each service's terms before you sign up.

Batch or subscription

When a quoted batch beats an unlimited subscription

A quoted batch fits when

  • You need a known set of ads by a date, such as the statics and videos for a launch, and want the total fixed before work starts.
  • You need video and product imagery as well as statics, and don't want to organize a shoot.
  • Your volume rises and falls through the year, so you'd otherwise pay for, or pause, a queue in quiet months.
  • Nobody on your team has time to brief and review requests every day.

A subscription fits when

  • You send small design requests every week across ads, email, social and decks.
  • Someone on your team writes the briefs and reviews work daily.
  • You want to add a request on any day without asking for a new quote.
  • Your budget is under $4,000, the smallest engagement 100 Creatives takes.
