Why a paid-social ad is a different job from the shelf

A pack is designed to be recognized from a few feet away among competitors on a shelf. A Meta or TikTok ad is seen on a phone, next to posts from friends, by someone who wasn't shopping. The same pack that wins the shelf can shrink to a thumbnail where the flavor name is unreadable.

That changes what the ad has to do. The pack needs to be large enough to read at phone size, or the headline has to carry the flavor for it. The ad needs one reason to stop, stated in the first line, because there's no aisle context telling the viewer what category they're looking at. And it needs variations: the same product shot against three backgrounds, four headlines and two crops, since the platform decides which one earns the spend. A branding agency usually delivers a handful of hero images per campaign. An ad creative partner builds the dozens of versions around them. Category rules that change the brief

CPG covers categories with very different ad rules, and the rule usually decides the concept before design starts.

Alcohol. Meta requires alcohol ads to follow local law and industry codes, and never to target anyone under 18. Some countries set a higher floor: 21 or older in the United States, 19 in Canada, 25 in Sweden. A few countries, Norway and Turkey among them, don't allow alcohol ads at all (Meta Transparency Center, Alcohol, checked September 2026). Promoting a brand, showing its logo and posting a cocktail recipe all count as alcohol ads under that policy.

Non-alcoholic drinks. The same policy allows ads for non-alcoholic beverages, as long as they don't depict alcohol or people drinking it. For a non-alcoholic wine or spirit brand, that shapes the whole concept. The product can be celebratory and look like the real thing on the table; the ad can't borrow a scene of someone drinking wine to make the point.

Supplements, functional food and wellness drinks. Anything that promises a health effect falls under FDA claim categories, FTC substantiation and Meta's 18+ rule for dietary and health products. Our supplement ad creative page covers structure/function claims, and health and wellness ad creative covers Meta's wellness rules in full.

Food, snacks and household. Fewer platform restrictions, more competition for attention. The limits here are the claims on your own pack: an ad can repeat "20g protein" if the label says it, and shouldn't add a number the label doesn't carry. Food and beverage ad creatives covers the image side of that rule, and the UK's 2026 limits on less healthy food ads.

Six CPG ad formats that work as statics

These are the formats we reach for first on a CPG brief, because each one gives a static a single job and each can be varied cheaply once it works.

1. The pack hero

The product, large, with one line of copy. It's the simplest ad and the one most brands under-produce, because a single studio shot gets reused until it fatigues. New backgrounds, props and crops around the same pack give the platform fresh versions without changing the message.

2. The flavor or variant lineup

Four flavors in a row, or the full range of sizes. Lineups answer "is there one for me?" and work well as the second ad a person sees after the pack hero. They're also where inconsistent product photography shows up fastest, since four packs shot on different days rarely match.

3. The usage moment

Where the product lives: yogurt on a breakfast counter, sparkling water in a cooler, a detergent pod going into the drum. Usage ads show the product in a real setting without implying a result, which keeps them safe in regulated categories.

4. The ingredient or sourcing story

What's in it and where it comes from, stated plainly: real fruit on the bottom, seeds roasted in-house, a short ingredient list. Only run what the pack or your documentation supports.

5. The where-to-buy ad

For brands with retail distribution, "Now at" or "Find it in the chilled aisle" with the retailer named, if the retailer allows its name in your ads. These push paid social toward in-store sales, which matters for brands whose online store is a small share of revenue.

6. The seasonal or limited run

A new flavor, a summer pack, a holiday bundle. The date or the season carries the urgency, so the ad doesn't need to invent any. A countdown to a sale that never ends is its own FTC problem.

Which format wins for a given product is a testing question. How many variations to run at a given spend is covered on how many ad creatives you need.

Product imagery without a shoot for every SKU

Most CPG ad programs stall on imagery. Every new flavor, size or seasonal pack needs photos, and booking a studio day for each one is slow. 100 Creatives makes its product and campaign images with Overs, an AI image tool that makes photos matching a brand without a shoot. Overs is our sister product, made by 100 Creatives' founder; both are brands of 2587689 Alberta Ltd.

It takes many tries per image. Before Overs existed, we built four campaigns by hand with AI image models and kept the records. A food campaign with motion took 120 renders to get 10 usable images. A gummy supplement campaign took 38 renders for 12. Across all four, 361 renders produced 45 keepers, about eight renders per usable photo. That ratio is why the review step matters, and why a pack with fine print or an unusual shape can take longer. Product visuals at scale covers how references, brand rules and review time work for a large catalog.

Some CPG ads still need a camera: a stain coming out in real time, a real customer's result, a person who has to be there. When you need a real photo shoot lists those cases, and we'll say so before quoting if your brief is one of them.

Frequently asked questions

What does a CPG creative agency do?
The term covers four kinds of shop. Branding and packaging agencies design the identity and the pack. Shopper and retail marketing agencies make shelf, end-cap and trade materials. Ad creative studios make the static and video ads a brand runs on Meta, TikTok and Google. Media agencies buy the placements. A few large agencies do all four, and most CPG brands end up hiring two or three specialists.
Is 100 Creatives a CPG branding agency?
No. 100 Creatives makes static ads for Meta, TikTok and Google, AI video ads and the product imagery behind them. It doesn't design packaging, brand identities, out-of-home or retail point-of-sale. A CPG brand that needs a new pack or a shelf launch should hire a branding or retail agency for that work, then bring the finished brand to an ad creative partner.
Can alcohol and non-alcoholic drink brands advertise on Meta?
Yes, with targeting rules. Meta requires alcohol ads to follow local law and never target anyone under 18, with higher minimums in some countries: 21 in the United States and 19 in Canada. Ads for non-alcoholic beverages are allowed as long as they do not depict alcohol or consumption (Meta Transparency Center, checked September 2026).
